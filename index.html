About

A career built one leadership step at a time.

I started as a programmer analyst at Mellon Bank, an individual contributor writing code. Within a few years I was leading the effort to build a new financial reporting system, the first time I led people, not just projects. That step repeated itself again and again over the next three decades. Team size grew from 5 to 40 to 65 to 160 across Allegheny Energy and, after its acquisition, FirstEnergy. Then came a smaller but truly global organization at Amicus Therapeutics, spanning four continents, where I picked up the international management experience the earlier roles hadn't required. Most recently, that's meant leading the organization's move into AI, championing Microsoft Copilot adoption and directing the build of an AI-driven service desk chatbot.

Each step meant taking on more complexity and more responsibility for people, not just for technology: negotiating contracts, resolving disputes, leading a business through a corporate acquisition, and turning failing operations around. At both FirstEnergy and Amicus, that responsibility extended well beyond direct headcount, leading much larger teams through outsourcing partnerships, which worked best when I treated the partner as a strategic collaborator rather than a vendor to manage. Some of the strongest working relationships of my career came out of that approach. I'm now offering that same leadership, built through three decades of doing it, as an independent advisor to organizations navigating similar decisions.
